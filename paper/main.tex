\begin{abstract}
An agent can preserve its memories while forgetting how to use them, and a locally
beneficial change can worsen the future state. We develop a continuation-aware
revision of an evidence-preserving online learner. The integrated reference learns
from its own executed transitions in a supplied finite deterministic environment
family, checks full-horizon Bellman advantages, preserves its previous certified
incumbent, and debits worst-case exploration loss before execution. We prove
conditional all-state improvement, retention within a truth-preserving contracting
model family, and a cumulative exploration-deficit bound. Separate components
study closed-state continuation equivalence, fixed-family noise-aware evidence,
immutable learned-feature modules, and coupled simulation. On 17,920 synthetic
episodes, the budgeted controller matches rather than beats a strong full-history
model-based learner's late return. A 384,000-interaction neural diagnostic shows
zero measured forgetting with immutable modules, but replay is a strong alternative
and versioning slightly lowers new-task accuracy. We report negative systems
results and explicit counterexamples. The release contains 271 passing Python
tests, 632 passing C++ cases plus an overflow check, 39 uncompiled Lean theorem
attempts, and uncompiled CUDA drafts. It does not establish a native language-agent
benchmark win, a general no-forgetting solution, or novelty of the combined design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and scope}
The intended deployed learner improves from a sequence of its own episodes without
an intervening offline retraining phase. The relevant target is transfer to later
problems with shared structure, not merely recalling a transcript or solving an
identical instance more often. CL-Bench reports that simple in-context learning
(ICL) outperforms dedicated memory systems in its evaluated configurations across
expert-validated stateful domains~\cite{clbench}. AgentCL separates controlled
compositional streams from naive and held-out streams; the latter can reveal
memory-induced degradation~\cite{agentcl}. Neither observation implies that online
adaptation never works or that ICL is universally optimal.

Our previous revision implements replay-safe rule growth, recurrence archives,
local one-outcome audits, and fixed-feature protected residuals. Its main unresolved
issues are continuation credit assignment, exploration loss before admission,
protection when features change, and the gap between retaining a rule and retaining
the complete deployed behavior. This revision addresses restricted forms of each
and records where the resulting mechanism fails to offer an empirical advantage.

The main integrated result is deliberately narrower than a general language agent.
We supply the possible finite worlds, but not the true world, and reveal only actual
executed transitions. The learner performs online identification, policy improvement,
and budgeted experiments. The primitive policy-improvement and concentration
arguments are classical. The proposed research direction is an explicit
continuation contract linking evidence, state, immutable versions, admission,
and execution cost. Its high-impact hypothesis remains unproven: that compact
contracts can be learned and reused cheaply enough to beat raw-history ICL.

\paragraph{What is unsolved.}
A practical learner must discover sufficient state, obtain feedback informative
about future consequences, propose useful changes, route them correctly under
recurrence or drift, and pay less for learning and verification than it gains in
future episodes. A supplied finite environment class bypasses the hardest
representation problem. We do not mistake its conditional theorem for an answer
to that acquisition problem.

\section{Related approaches}
\paragraph{Context and memory.}
Raw-history ICL is a mandatory comparator because it retains information that a
premature summary may discard. ACE evolves structured playbooks~\cite{ace};
ALMA searches executable memory designs through a meta-agent~\cite{alma}.
These approaches concern what is stored, retrieved, and updated, but memory
quality alone does not certify downstream behavioral preservation. Meta-design
search also has a cost that must be separated from strictly online deployment.
AgentCL's MemProbe stores interactions, insights, and skills with a reliability
filter, making it a stronger controlled-transfer comparator than an arbitrary
retrieval baseline~\cite{agentcl}.

\paragraph{Harness evolution.}
EvoTest updates the agent's prompt, memory, parameters of the harness, and tool-use
routines after episodes without weight fine-tuning~\cite{evotest}. TTHE evolves
executable harnesses during evaluation and commits using execution-derived proxy
signals; proxy reliability is an explicit challenge~\cite{tthe}. Decision-Aware
Memory Cards focuses selection on downstream action utility rather than generic
relevance~\cite{cards}. Our continuation framing does not establish superiority
over any of these implementations. It asks for a stronger accounting of persistent
state and delayed consequences than a next-action or proxy-score comparison alone.

\paragraph{Conservative learning and neural retention.}
SPIBB bootstraps from a baseline in uncertain regions using a fixed dataset~\cite{spibb}.
StepMix and EpsMix study conservative exploration with episode-wise guarantees
in tabular MDPs~\cite{stepmix}. DeepSPI already connects online safe policy
improvement, world models, and representation learning, with evaluation on
ALE-57~\cite{deepspi}. These are direct prior-art constraints: our supplied-world
result is narrower, not a first theorem for safe online learning. None of these
settings alone establishes retention of heterogeneous language-agent skills under
changing memory and routing. Accordingly, our budget is not the first safe
exploration mechanism. Progressive neural networks already exploit frozen
previous columns~\cite{progressive}; gradient projection protects specified
functional directions~\cite{ogd}. We use isolation to address a concrete limitation
of a saturated fixed-feature protected subspace, not to claim invention of
architectural non-forgetting. Time-uniform confidence methods are likewise
established~\cite{confidence}.

\section{Setting and four retention notions}
Let $\M$ be a finite family of stationary deterministic environments with finite
states $S$, actions $A$, and horizon $H$. World $m$ has transition
$T_m(t,s,a)$ and integer reward $r_m(t,s,a)$. Time and the complete state are
observable; the true world index $m^*$ is unknown. Episode boundaries are genuine
resets. Every policy $p$ maps $(t,s)$ to an action and has remaining value
\begin{align}
 V_m^p(H,s)&=0,\\
 V_m^p(t,s)&=r_m(t,s,p(t,s))\notag\\
 &\quad+V_m^p(t+1,T_m(t,s,p(t,s))).\label{eq:value}
\end{align}
The original immutable baseline is $b_0$. The consistency family $\C_n$ contains
worlds agreeing with every actually observed reward and successor transition.
Truth membership $m^*\in\C_n$ follows by induction if the family was initially
realizable and observations are exact. This is an assumption, not an empirical
calibration claim.

We distinguish four protections. \emph{Evidence retention} preserves observations.
\emph{Function retention} preserves a stored module with unchanged input
construction. \emph{Incumbent retention} protects the performance of already
certified policies. \emph{Deployment retention} must also account for the router,
exploration, hidden controller state, and the future environment. The first three
alone do not imply the fourth.

\paragraph{Class growth and contradiction.}
A constraint redundant in the old class need not be redundant after adding new
models. All historical witnesses are therefore replayed on every new model.
An empty $\C_n$ is a contradiction, never a vacuous certificate. A model-family
expansion can invalidate a previous policy comparison and begins a separately
marked trust era. Under hidden drift, an adverse outcome may occur before its
first contradiction. Detection is not retroactive prevention.

\section{Continuation-aware admission}
\subsection{Full state, not just matching actions}
Let a complete system transition be $F(z,u)=(z',o)$, where $z$ includes environment
state, controller memory, selected module, input normalization, relevant tool state,
and random-generator state; $u$ is an exogenous input. Define $G$ for a replacement
system on the same state space.

\begin{theorem}[Closed continuation equality]\label{thm:closed}
If a protected predicate $P$ satisfies, for every $z\in P$ and permitted $u$,
$F(z,u)=G(z,u)$ and $F(z,u)_1\in P$, then every finite input sequence produces
the same output trace and final complete state under $F$ and $G$, when started
from the same state in $P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The empty sequence is immediate. Equality gives the same first output and
successor state. Closure places that successor in $P$, so induction applies to the
remaining input sequence.
\end{proof}

The condition is strong but precise. Equal current actions do not imply equal
controller-memory updates. Two controllers can agree now and diverge on the next
action. A finite test set is not a proof of closure on an unrestricted state space;
the executable finite-domain checker requires an enumerated protected domain.

\subsection{Robust Bellman obligations}
For incumbent $b$, define the continuation-aware advantage of action $a$:
\begin{align}
 A_m^b(t,s,a)&=r_m(t,s,a)\notag\\
 &\quad+V_m^b(t+1,T_m(t,s,a))-V_m^b(t,s).
\end{align}
A candidate policy $c$ passes if
\begin{equation}
 \min_{m\in\C_n} A_m^b(t,s,c(t,s))\geq0
 \qquad\text{for every }t,s.\label{eq:admit}
\end{equation}
The incumbent's own action is always admissible. The implementation maximizes
summed model advantages among admissible actions and prefers the incumbent on
ties. That ranking is a heuristic; the guarantee uses the minimum.

\begin{theorem}[All-state improvement]\label{thm:improve}
If $\C_n\ne\varnothing$ and~\eqref{eq:admit} holds, then
$V_m^c(t,s)\geq V_m^b(t,s)$ for every $m\in\C_n,t,s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Terminal values agree. At an earlier stage, apply induction at the candidate's
successor and then~\eqref{eq:admit}:
\begin{align*}
 V_m^c(t,s)&=r_m(t,s,c)+V_m^c(t+1,T_m(t,s,c))\\
 &\geq r_m(t,s,c)+V_m^b(t+1,T_m(t,s,c))\\
 &\geq V_m^b(t,s).
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:improve} is classical policy-improvement reasoning. It is a
sufficient, not necessary, admission rule. Some coordinated policy changes improve
full value even though an intermediate action has negative advantage relative to
the old continuation. More expressive policy-level certificates could admit them.
An average-return criterion would be cheaper but could sacrifice a protected rare
state, so it is not substituted for the stated all-state guarantee.

\subsection{Protect acquired gains, not only the anchor}
Within a contracting family, every previous universal obligation remains true.
Consequently, each new improvement round begins from the \emph{previous certified
incumbent}. By transitivity and truth membership, its true all-state value is
nondecreasing. Rebuilding from $b_0$ each episode protects only the original floor;
it can forget an earlier improvement. We found and corrected precisely that bug
while developing this revision, then added update-by-update regression tests.

Contracts bind the baseline hash, candidate hash, model-family identities, and
generation. A changed encoder, model family, baseline, or scope requires a new
contract. The implementation rejects stale or foreign certificates. After new
plausible worlds are admitted, it starts a new trust era from $b_0$ rather than
claiming that earlier incumbent guarantees extend to a larger family.

\section{Accounting for exploration}
A safe incumbent need not generate identifying evidence. We therefore examine
candidate episode policies from surviving model optima and the immutable archive.
For candidate $p$ at actual initial state $s_n$, reserve
\begin{equation}
 D_n(p)=\max\left\{0,\max_{m\in\C_n}
 [V_m^{b_n}(0,s_n)-V_m^p(0,s_n)]\right\}.\label{eq:debit}
\end{equation}
Run the trial only if past debits plus $D_n(p)$ do not exceed the declared budget
$B$. Reserve this debit \emph{before} execution and never refund it merely because
the realized trial was favorable. Among feasible trials, the reference prioritizes
worst-case reduction in the plausible-family size, then optimistic gain and lower
debit. Predicted trajectories are computed from supplied models; only the actual
executed trajectory is admitted as evidence.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\small
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{Family $\C$, incumbent $b$, anchor $b_0$, archive, spent budget $L$, cap $B$}
Replay new observed transitions; reject certification if $\C=\varnothing$\;
If plausible models were added, start a new trust era with $b\leftarrow b_0$\;
Improve $b$ using all-model, all-state Bellman obligations\;
Construct candidate trials from model optima and the archive\;
Compute predicted signatures and $D(p)$; discard trials with $L+D(p)>B$\;
Select an informative feasible trial $p$, or use $b$\;
Reserve $L\leftarrow L+D(p)$; execute $p$ once\;
Record the actual complete trajectory; update $\C$ and archive\;
\caption{Deterministic continuation-contract learner. No step uses the evaluator's true-world index.}
\label{alg:main}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Completed-prefix deficit]\label{thm:budget}
In a stationary realizable deterministic era with genuine episode resets,
Algorithm~\ref{alg:main} satisfies, at every completed episode prefix $N$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n=1}^N\big[V_{m^*}^{b_0}(0,s_n)-R_n\big]
 \leq\sum_{n=1}^ND_n\leq B.\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Incumbent improvement gives $V^{b_0}\leq V^{b_n}$. Truth membership and the
maximization in~\eqref{eq:debit} give $V^{b_n}-V^{p_n}\leq D_n$ at $s_n$.
Deterministic execution gives $R_n=V^{p_n}$. Add these inequalities. The admission
ledger ensures the second inequality.
\end{proof}

Initial states need not be independently sampled. They must be the same actual
states used for the baseline comparison. With cross-episode carryover,
Equation~\eqref{eq:budget} is not a comparison to the baseline's counterfactual
lifelong trajectory: prior actions may change later starts. One must include the
missing continuation value or prove regeneration before making that stronger
claim. The budget also does not prohibit individual adverse actions, privacy
violations, or irreversible real-world harm.

\begin{proposition}[A zero-risk obstruction]
Consider two observationally indistinguishable worlds in which the baseline
always returns $5$, while a trial returns $0$ in one and $10$ in the other.
No algorithm required to have zero expected deficit in both worlds can assign
positive trial probability before obtaining distinguishing information.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A trial probability $p>0$ produces expected deficit $5p$ in the unfavorable world.
Baseline observations cannot distinguish the worlds. Thus universal zero expected
deficit requires $p=0$, preventing strict improvement in the favorable world.
\end{proof}

This does not end the research problem. It identifies the resource that must be
provided: safe informative actions, a nonzero exposure allowance, trusted side
information, or a legally available simulator.

\section{Independent extensions}
\subsection{Fixed-family noise-aware evidence}
Hard deletion is incorrect when valid hypotheses sometimes produce an unexpected
outcome. For a fixed finite probabilistic family and prior weights $\pi_m>0$
chosen before data, let $L_{m,n}$ be the likelihood of actual observations under
predictable chosen actions and let $L_{\mathrm{mix},n}=\sum_m\pi_mL_{m,n}$.
The ratio $E_{m,n}=L_{\mathrm{mix},n}/L_{m,n}$ is a nonnegative supermartingale
under true $m$; common-support conditions give martingale equality. Standard
maximal-inequality reasoning yields~\cite{confidence}
\begin{equation}
 \PP_m\{\exists n:E_{m,n}\geq1/\delta\}\leq\delta.
\end{equation}
The exact-rational reference retains $m$ when $\delta L_{\mathrm{mix},n}<L_{m,n}$.
Tests enumerate all binary histories of length six and verify the likelihood-ratio
expectation exactly in a common-support example.

This does not justify assigning a new data-dependent model retrospective
likelihood credit. Nor does it turn an uncalibrated neural score into a likelihood.
The filter is not integrated into the deterministic controller. A stochastic
controller would additionally need expected-value backups and a valid allowance
for realized-return deviations. It cannot inherit~\eqref{eq:budget} by renaming a
consistency set a confidence set.

\subsection{Learning features without overwriting them}
The earlier fixed-feature residual protects an old feature span but loses freedom
when that span fills the representation. The new neural component trains both
features and a head in a separate module, then freezes the resulting arrays as
immutable bytes. Old public-schema dispatch and old input construction do not
change. Accordingly, the old function is unchanged for every input still routed
to that module. This is architectural isolation, related to progressive
networks~\cite{progressive}, with growing storage rather than a fixed-memory
solution. Functional preservation becomes full behavioral preservation only when
the closed-state conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:closed} are also satisfied.

The executed diagnostic has two exhaustive actions and exact success feedback.
Given the chosen action, success or failure determines the binary target. This
own-feedback supervision argument is invalid for noisy outcomes or arbitrary
multi-action tasks. Every baseline receives the same public scope tag. No latent
scope inference, LLM weight update, or general transfer result is established.

\subsection{Coupled simulator execution}
The paired simulator advances two controller memories independently under a shared
exogenous noise tape. It shares one environment transition exactly when the two
actual states and actions agree, otherwise evaluates both. Actual reconvergence
can permit later sharing; matching now never licenses skipping an unchecked suffix.
For $H$ steps and $L$ shared transitions, the environment-call count is $2H-L$
instead of $2H$, while controller calls remain $2H$. This is a semantic work-saving
identity, not a claim that arbitrary external tools can be cloned or that LLM
serving becomes proportionally faster.

\section{Experiments and negative results}
\subsection{Stateful online learning}
We ran twenty independent seeds, sixty-four episodes, seven arms, and two finite
world families: delayed damage and compositional navigation. Each has $H=5$,
$|S|=4$, $|A|=3$, and a supplied family constructed from sixteen latent masks;
semantically duplicate worlds are deduplicated. The hidden mask is not given to
the learner. Episodes reset and initial states cycle through the public state
space. All methods receive the same actual feedback protocol. The complete
17,920 episode records are archived with seed-level summaries.

Table~\ref{tab:continuation} supports the continuation mechanism but rejects a
performance-breakthrough interpretation. In delayed damage, immediate greedy
control has worst cumulative deficit $133$, versus $2$ for $B=4$. However, the
strong full-history baseline also has worst deficit $2$ and reaches exactly the
same late return as $B=4$ on every seed in both domains. The paired late-return
difference is identically zero in this study. Zero budget reduces delayed-damage
late return from $13.1375$ to $12.5625$. The contract controller is also slower on
these small CPU problems because it performs additional all-state and trial
planning work. Its guarantee, rather than a measured performance advantage, is
the positive result here.

The delayed-damage seed-level late-return standard deviation is $0.620$ for the
full-history and $B=4$ methods, and $0.628$ for $B=0$. Episodes are correlated
within a seed; treating 17,920 rows as independent observations would overstate
statistical evidence. Generalization is limited by two small generated families,
supplied model structure, exact feedback, and short horizons.

\subsection{Online feature learning and retention}
Twenty seeds cover three public scopes with 1,600 feedback examples each and four

tag. Each scope has 512 independent held-out probes. New-task online accuracy is
measured before each update. Forgetting is the acquired held-out accuracy minus
later accuracy, averaged across old-scope checkpoints. No separate offline training

\subsection{Noise, sharing, and kernel costs}
The noise experiment draws 1,000 independently seeded length-64 sequences with
$10\%$ noise and $\delta=0.05$. The fixed-family likelihood filter ever excludes
truth in 12 sequences ($1.2\%$); hard deletion loses truth in 997 ($99.7\%$).
The finite test does not prove coverage. Correct likelihood specification remains
an explicit premise of the probability argument.

Across 200 paired simulator tests of horizon 32, every coupled trace matches its
independent reference. Mean environment calls fall from 64 to $39.405$, a $38.43\%$
reduction. Controller calls remain 64. Late-divergence construction favors sharing;
this does not estimate savings in real tool or LLM workloads.

The packed NumPy continuation pass has essentially no cold end-to-end advantage
in the measured shapes. At $M=4,16,64$, scalar times are $0.885,2.792,9.830$ ms,
versus packed $0.809,2.725,10.024$ ms. Medians include table construction and value
computation. Packing is slightly slower at 64 models. Device-resident caching may
change that outcome, but no such measurement exists in this release.

\section{Engineering and verification}
One dense all-model, all-action advantage pass costs $O(MHSA)$, with model storage
of the same order. The implementation evaluates up to the number of unique model
optima plus archived candidates; scoring $K$ candidates by predicted signatures
costs $O(KMH)$ after their construction, and value computations add work. Repeated
policy-improvement passes multiply admission cost. Model-family enumeration is
not cheap simply because its arithmetic can be vectorized.

The checked C++ scalar reference uses wide intermediates and rejects int64
overflow. The NumPy primitive rejects unsafe magnitude bounds before arithmetic.
The CUDA draft proposes one 256-thread block per $(t,s,a)$ to reduce across model
advantages. It assumes valid indices, a positive model count, matching immutable
model/value snapshots, and the documented integer bound. There is no executed
host binding, sanitizer result, or GPU timing for that draft. A useful next
optimization would retain immutable tables and generation-indexed value caches
on device, but only if profiling shows this work matters after model fitting,
LLM inference, and tool execution are included.

\paragraph{Validation status.}
All 271 Python tests pass, including delayed harm, hidden controller divergence,
empty-family rejection, stale certificates, class expansion, prefix deficits,
incumbent retention, noisy evidence, immutable arrays, and scalar/vector parity.
All 432 legacy C++ cases and 200 new randomized cases pass, plus an explicit
overflow rejection test. The new C++ test runs with undefined-behavior checking.
These observations are not proofs of unrestricted implementation correctness.

The repository has 39 Lean theorem declarations: thirteen original, sixteen in
the prior refinement, and ten continuation declarations. All are attempts, not
kernel-checked theorems. The local build fails because \texttt{lake} is absent,
and the official toolchain download attempt also fails. The new source addresses
closed traces, Bellman comparison, truth membership, shrinking obligations,
integer debit addition, a zero-risk obstruction, and incumbent ordering. It does
not formalize stochastic coverage, floating point, Python refinement, or CUDA.
The included \texttt{\#print axioms} commands are unexecuted source, not evidence
that a compiler checked their dependencies.

\section{Learning compact abstractions}
The strongest untested extension is counterexample-guided abstraction refinement.
A deployed learner would construct a typed abstract state from observable schema
and raw witnesses, synthesize a skill, and split abstract states when different
future consequences were incorrectly merged. Earlier abstractions and modules
remain versioned. New candidates need either a uniform model-error certificate
or an independent prospective full-continuation audit. Training fit alone is not
such a certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform approximate-model margin]\label{thm:approx}
Suppose a true and abstract finite-horizon model share a valid policy/state
correspondence. Both reward functions lie in $[0,R_{\max}]$, their absolute
reward discrepancy is at most $\epsilon_r$, and their one-step transition total
variation is at most $\epsilon_p$, uniformly on all relevant state--action pairs.
For any fixed policy with $h$ remaining steps, the value error is at most
\begin{equation}
 E_h=h\epsilon_r+R_{\max}\epsilon_p\frac{h(h-1)}2.
\end{equation}
An abstract candidate-minus-baseline advantage of at least $2E_h$ therefore
implies nonnegative true value difference at that state.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The terminal error is zero. Each recursion adds at most $\epsilon_r$ reward
error, $E_{h-1}$ successor value error, and
$\epsilon_p(h-1)R_{\max}$ distribution error, because the successor value span
is at most $(h-1)R_{\max}$. Summing proves $E_h$. Apply the error bound to both
policies and subtract. Adaptive policy selection requires a simultaneous envelope
covering all policies that may be selected.
\end{proof}

This is a useful rejection rule, not a claim to have learned the required
uniform envelope. Partial observability can make an abstraction non-Markovian;
rare states can invalidate empirical coverage; longer horizons inflate the bound
quadratically; and conservative margins can reject nearly every useful change.
The first experiment should hide the generator family from the learner while
keeping the exact world available to the evaluator, then independently vary
aliasing, delayed rewards, horizon, grammar depth, and misspecification. Compare
learned abstractions with supplied models, full-history planning, and a real
retrieval-plus-LLM learner on held-out composition and recurrence.

\paragraph{Memory cannot be free.}
After $N$ independent binary facts, exact answers to arbitrary old-fact queries
require distinguishing $2^N$ possible histories, hence at least $N$ bits of system
state. Bounded active memory plus an external archive is compatible with this
limit; uniformly bounded total storage and unlimited exact arbitrary recall are
not. Learned abstractions must compress reusable structure, not claim to compress
independent information without loss.

\section{External evaluation plan}
No native CL-Bench, AgentCL, J-TTL, model-server, or GH200 run was executed. The
current CL-Bench ICL source was inspected: it provides respond/observe/reset,
usage logging, and FIFO context handling, but not the finite environment family
required by our controller. A generic wrapper is not a completed native adapter.
The repository records inspected file hashes and an integration protocol.

Use two fixed backbones, all six CL-Bench domains, and AgentCL's controlled,
naive, and held-out streams. Mandatory applicable comparators include stateless,
raw-history ICL, scratchpad ICL, ACE, Mem0, MemProbe, harness evolution, v0.2,
and the proposed method. Charge all generated candidates, judges, replay,
online updates, model fitting, failed verification, and simulator work. Compare
both equal token/cost budgets and equal wall time. Use at least twenty paired
independent seeds per domain where allowed and resample whole streams, not
correlated episodes. A development-only power pilot should set a final sample
size before test inspection.

The proposed practical target is at least five percentage points higher native
learning gain, a positive paired 95\% confidence lower bound in the prespecified
pooled comparison, positive point estimates on at least four of six CL-Bench
domains, and no protected domain regression beyond two percentage points. These
are chosen research thresholds, not consequences of our theorems. Simultaneous
per-domain claims require appropriate uncertainty correction. A nonsignificant
loss is not evidence of noninferiority.

Kill the claimed learning advantage if the strongest cost-matched baseline
matches or wins inside the prespecified practical-equivalence margin. Kill the
abstraction mechanism if honest coverage rejects over 90\% of otherwise useful
proposals, state size grows with raw history rather than structure, or held-out
aliasing breaks a claimed guarantee. Kill the systems speed claim if it disappears
after packing, model fitting, launches, and cache management are charged. Any
reproducible exact mismatch ends the associated correctness claim immediately.

\paragraph{Resources.}
The included mechanisms require CPU, NumPy, pytest, and a C++17 compiler. A single
GH200 is a proposed open-model test platform, subject to the selected model's
actual weight, cache, activation, optimizer, and module footprint. Native CL-Bench
documents Python 3.13+, \texttt{uv}, and Docker. Paired execution requires legal,
read-only snapshotable workspaces. As an explicit planning example, six domains,
two backbones, seven arms, twenty seeds, and sixty-four episodes yield 107,520
agent episodes before extra candidates. At an assumed 20,000 processed tokens
per episode, this is 2.1504 billion tokens. Neither this assumption nor any actual
GPU speed or native run duration was measured; use a pilot for resource budgeting.

\section{Conclusion}
This revision turns vague retention language into specific continuation,
incumbent, and exploration contracts. The finite-world learner improves online
without an offline retraining phase and has conditional retention and deficit
proofs. The experiments support the mechanisms but do not beat a strong
full-history model-based baseline, establish a cost-free neural retention gain,
or demonstrate native language-agent superiority. The consequential open task is
learning compact, reliable abstractions and useful reusable programs whose
verification costs less than the future value they create. The artifact makes
that hypothesis executable and falsifiable rather than presenting it as solved.
